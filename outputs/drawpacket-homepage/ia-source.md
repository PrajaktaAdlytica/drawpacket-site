# Research findings and structural lessons

Research used the configured Perplexity high research tool, followed by direct official-page checks. Findings describe retrieved page content and link structure; no competitor animation or working app behavior was visually tested. Levelset’s direct follow-up fetch returned 403; its content summary below relies on the Perplexity retrieval. Vendor claims establish existing capability overlap, not independent proof of efficacy.

| Reference | Observed structure | DrawPacket decision |
|---|---|---|
| [GCPay features](https://ww3.gcpay.com/features/) | Feature navigation separates workflow, waivers, compliance, integrations, payments and reporting; solutions, resources, pricing and demo paths surround it | Keep five offers discoverable and individually explained; retain useful detail routes |
| [Procore Pay](https://www.procore.com/pay) | Product promise → platform fit → payment requirements → waiver exchange → approval workflow → customer proof/integrations → demo | Explain evidence and human decisions before commercial CTA; do not borrow live payment or integration claims |
| [Levelset waiver management](https://www.levelset.com/lien-waiver-management/) | Waiver-focused explanation, tiers, collection/exchange, education and conversion | Give waiver relationships their own module and sourced education page |
| [Linear](https://linear.app/) | Detailed product UI leads into intake, planning and building workflow chapters; product/resources/pricing navigation | Use a continuing, legible record and meaningful chapter progression; original visual design |
| [Ramp](https://ramp.com/) | Central finance promise, workflow/use-case sections and repeated conversion routes | Let visitors convert after understanding a relevant workflow without completing the entire story |
| [Built draw management](https://getbuilt.com/features/draw-management/) | Narrow workflow page within a wider construction-finance site, explaining draw collaboration and status | Keep the initial project/cycle scope visible; owner/lender packet workflows are established territory |

**Competitive conclusion:** none of the five offers can be called exclusive on this evidence. GCPay, Procore, Levelset, accounting/PM tools, title/escrow administration and email/spreadsheets are alternatives or substitutes. Incremental value must be measured against the GC’s actual stack. An evidence-linked reviewer experience is a positioning hypothesis, not proven differentiation.

**Workflow conclusion:** pay applications include line-item work, stored materials, changes and supporting records, with contract-specific requirements. This supports a source-linked reconciliation story rather than generic automation imagery. [Procore payment-application guide](https://www.procore.com/library/construction-payment-applications); [AIA G703 instructions](https://help.aiacontracts.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500009308302-instructions-g703-1992-continuation-sheet).

**Waiver conclusion:** California’s official guidance distinguishes conditional/unconditional and progress/final forms. Conditional release depends on payment; an unconditional form asserts receipt. Signatures and document receipt must not stand in for payment evidence. California is an example only; project jurisdiction and approved contract/form requirements govern the pilot. [California CSLB waiver guidance](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/consumers/legal_issues_for_consumers/mechanics_lien/conditional_and_unconditional_waiver_release_form.aspx).

**Insurance conclusion:** date and document review must be separate from determining coverage. The ACORD reference describes a certificate as evidence of policy information, not the policy itself; the direct follow-up fetch was unavailable, so validate its current wording before publishing insurance education. [ACORD certificates FAQ](https://www.acord.org/docs/default-source/forms/acordcertificatesfaq). No DrawPacket screen should claim “coverage verified.”

**Motion conclusion:** give nonessential interaction motion a reduced-motion path; provide pause/stop/hide for qualifying automatic movement. Animation-from-interactions is WCAG AAA guidance; the site can adopt it alongside an AA target. [W3C animation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html); [W3C pause guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html).

The supplied brief rates workflow/substitute evidence high and incremental automation value low. This research supports extensive incumbent overlap but does not establish willingness to pay, avoided delay, insurer/lender acceptance or a validated market gap. Interview and pilot questions must address those gaps.

# Pilot pricing and validation plan

Public commercial copy:
“Test one project. Review one monthly cycle. Proposed diagnostic: $2,000 fixed. Proposed supervised project/cycle pilot: $1,500–$4,000. These are hypothetical test prices, not current offers or accepted market pricing. Scope and price would be agreed before a pilot.”

The diagnostic and pilot are alternatives to scope, not automatically additive fees or tiers. No checkout, card capture or payment collection. Each proposal specifies one chosen module, project, cycle, permitted data, template set, responsible AP owner, reviewers, deliverables and decision date.

Demand test:

1. Recruit through construction accountants/CPAs, Procore integrators, subcontractor networks and regional construction associations.

2. Shadow an actual GC draw cycle with permission. Map present tools, handoffs, return reasons and owner/lender requirements.

3. Manually inspect a permissioned de-identified sample against GC and contract/form rules. Establish avoidable returns, legitimate holds, omissions and actual review time.

4. Agree a supervised paid project pilot, template access and named AP owner. Maintain existing authorization and payment process.

5. Compare the selected module against reviewed ground truth, including subcontractor burden and incumbents’ existing coverage.

Pilot scorecard:

- True avoidable returns: GC adjudicates each return and whether the proposed review would have prevented it. Define return unit and reason codes before testing.

- Review time: minutes per draw, measured consistently, including preparation, verification and correction time; compare like scope and volume.

- Wrongful holds: proposed holds judged unsupported by the approved rule/evidence.

- Material omissions: relevant holds or evidence gaps missed by review.

- Subcontractor burden: follow-ups, duplicate submissions and response time, compared with current process.

- Acceptance: actual owner/lender/insurer feedback where relevant; no inference from a polished export.

- Commitment: signed, funded one-project scope with AP owner and template/data permission.

**Go only if** the supervised cycle demonstrates fewer true avoidable returns without wrongful holds, no unresolved material safety/payment evidence omission, approved state-specific templates and a funded project owner. Agree exact measurement windows and thresholds with the GC before the pilot; report counts and denominators, not a self-scored AI-accuracy percentage. A single pilot is local evidence, not a generalized ROI result.

**Pause and correct** when sample permissions, source versions, reviewer ownership or ground truth are incomplete. **Stop** if waiver complexity, payment liability, participation burden or incumbent coverage removes the incremental gain; if material omissions persist; or if the owner/lender rejects the packet for the intended use.

Expansion is conditional: repeat across a GC portfolio, reuse permissioned subcontractor records, then explore onboarding and lender/owner reporting only after integration and participation value are demonstrated. “Million-dollar product” remains an internal hypothesis, never homepage copy.
